Research objectives

Apollo lets people recover access to an encrypted vault with help from trusted contacts. I explored two questions: how recovery can start when the user is unavailable, and how well people understand the setup, backup, and recovery workflows.

Delegated recovery

I designed and implemented a Go extension that lets a quorum of delegates authorize recovery when the user is unavailable. Delegates approve the request and trustees provide the underlying recovery material.

I evaluated the implementation across 19 correctness scenarios, measured verification latency, and estimated communication overhead. I used 10,000 Monte Carlo trials per configuration to study quorum completion and delegate discovery.

With five delegates and a threshold of three, the estimated probability of forming a quorum was 99.63%, conditional on one delegate already having initiated recovery and a 90% independent response probability for each remaining delegate. Delegate discovery was the main operational bottleneck.

Usability pilot

Ten participants tried setup, backup, and recovery on a mobile or web prototype. Many could navigate the workflow but still had questions about backup distribution, thresholds, and the roles of selected contacts. The pilot helped identify what to examine in the larger Apollo usability study.
