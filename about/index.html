Interests

Nothing very realistic for the first two topics, just some thoughts mixing sci-fi, hope and imagination (otherwise, what's the point?).

Astronomy & Space

Initially, I wanted a career in astronomy, to become an astronaut, go into astrophysics or even build rockets. It's a field that fascinates me and that I've been immersed in since childhood, mainly through books. What intrigued me so much about it was the unknown.

Take the game Prey, or even better, the movie Arrival. I think it's one of the best movies I've ever seen, and the one that best explains why I'm so drawn to the unknown: a mix of curiosity and hype at the prospect of discovering something that would completely challenge our understanding of the world. That's somewhat what the unknown of space makes me feel. I think it left such an impression on me partly because of how intense those feelings are.

I also immersed myself in other works of science fiction, particularly Star Wars and the comics Les Mondes d'Aldébaran (I should also watch Dune).

All of these really made me want humanity to have a fair shot at colonizing space. I don't know if I'm too optimistic or idealistic about this. It's possible that I am. It's also possible that we're still too early in our history to know whether it's realistic or not. I've often been told it isn't.

I'd like to live longer to find out. I'd like a lot of things.

Transhumanism & human enhancement

Transhumanism is so cool. Growing up, my dad introduced me to Terminator. I then discovered Cyberpunk on my own, along with a few episodes of Black Mirror. I may have gotten carried away by hype and hope, as I often do, but some (very far away) of the possibilities transhumanism explores fascinate me.

One of my biggest fears is neurodegen diseases, and the idea that maybe one day we might be better at preventing or treating them gives me a lot of hope. I mentioned earlier that I'd like to live longer (like everyone else, cpt. obvious), and the possibility of pushing some of our biological limits feels so far away and yet so possible (?).

I also thought about augmented humans, smarter and sharper humans, and what they could accomplish.

But would that necessarily be a good thing? Would things still have the same meaning? Wouldn't we lack intellectual stimulation?

It's like the Qu in All Tomorrows or Gaia in Foundation: imagine ending up as a collective intelligence that would represent the pinnacle of intelligence, but what's the point if we're no longer individuals?

Math & Strategy

This one is much more down to earth than the other two.

I first became interested in this through a few courses at EPFL: Algorithms I and AICC I/II. I then spent quite a while looking for concrete and fun applications.

I think what really makes me enjoy this is this very intuitive way of solving problems. We don't always use particularly complex tools, yet we manage to solve problems that are.

I've seen this a lot: competition in games. Whether in StarCraft II or chess, it's always been about being one step ahead, using your intuition and thinking different. I also like being able to exploit the uncertainty of human behavior. Take certain gambits in chess: a move doesn't necessarily have to be objectively the best one to be the most interesting one to play against a human opponent.

I think this is really something that defines the way I think and explains why I find so much enjoyment in solving certain problems.

It's unique and I love it.
